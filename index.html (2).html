Program Introduction

Overview, Program Standards, & AMA 11th Edition Foundation

This manual serves as the official student guide for written assignments, doctoral manuscripts, and research papers within the University of Lynchburg Doctor of Medical Science (DMSc) Program. It outlines mandatory program layout standards, American Medical Association Manual of Style 11th edition (AMA) guidelines, academic writing expectations, and common grammatical conventions.

Standard Hierarchy Notice

This guide is aligned directly with the AMA Manual of Style (11th edition). Where the official AMA Manual is silent regarding specific academic coursework formatting (e.g., title pages, course headers, or grading margins), the DMSc program standards detailed herein take precedence.

DMSc Manuscript Preparation (AMA Ch. 2.0)

Formatting Specs, Order of Components, & Mandatory AI Disclosures

Standard Font Arial 12pt (or Times New Roman 12pt)
Margins 1.0 Inch All sides (ragged right)
Spacing Double-Spaced 0.5 in paragraph indent
Pagination Upper Right Starts on Title Page

Required Sequence of Submission Elements

  1. 1 Title Page
  2. 2 Abstract (if required)
  3. 3 Body Text
  4. 4 Acknowledgment & AI Disclosure
  5. 5 Reference List
  6. 6 Tables / Figures / Appendices

Mandatory AI Disclosure Statement Requirement

Required

Every DMSc assignment must include an explicit Artificial Intelligence disclosure in the Acknowledgment section. Select and copy the appropriate template below:

Template A: AI Tools Utilized 
Google Docs spelling assistant was used to identify editing errors. OpenAI ChatGPT (Model GPT-4) was queried on October 12, 2026, to assist in structuring topic outlines. The author reviewed, revised, and takes full responsibility for all final manuscript content.
Template B: No AI Tools Utilized 
All research, drafting, and editing for this submission were conducted entirely by the author without the use of artificial intelligence software or generative language models.

Academic Writing Expectations

Clinical Tone, Diction, Tense Standards, & Paraphrasing Rules

Doctoral writing demands concise, objective, and evidence-backed communication. Clinical papers should prioritize precision over decorative language.

Pillar Definition Scientific Expectation
Diction Specific word choice and domain vocabulary. Use precise medical terminology; avoid slang, idioms, and casual expressions.
Syntax Sentence structure, length, and rhythm. Keep sentences direct and concise (aiming for 12–18 words per sentence).
Tone The objective stance toward subject matter. Maintain a neutral, evidence-driven perspective; avoid sensationalism.

Encouraged Best Practices

  • Paraphrase sources accurately while citing the original study.
  • Use past tense when describing methods or past trial results.
  • Use present tense when discussing established medical truths or general facts.

Strict Avoidance Rules

  • Direct block quotes (unless examining historical or legal verbatim text).
  • Emotional modifiers, exaggeration, and dramatic flair.
  • First-person personal opinions ("I feel that...", "In my opinion...").

Grammar and Punctuation

Oxford Comma, Period Rules, Colons, & Hyphenation

Mandatory Oxford (Serial) Comma Standard

The DMSc program enforces the mandatory use of the Oxford (serial) comma preceding the final conjunction in any series of 3 or more items to prevent clinical ambiguity.

✕ Incorrect (Ambiguous): The PA evaluated heart rate, oxygen saturation and blood pressure.
✓ Correct (DMSc Program Standard): The PA evaluated heart rate, oxygen saturation, and blood pressure.

Periods in Abbreviations

AMA style omits periods in most abbreviations and acronyms (e.g., write CDC, NIH, USA, VA, not C.D.C. or V.A.).

Colons in Section Headings

Do not end section headings or standalone subheadings with a colon unless followed directly by text on the same line.

Capitalization & Italics Rules

Titles, Professional Titles, Drug Names, & Organisms

Drug & Medication Names

Generic names: Lowercase (e.g., metformin, acetaminophen, lisinopril).

Proprietary/Trade names: Capitalized (e.g., Glucophage, Tylenol, Zestril).

Organism & Genus Species

Italics required for binomial species names (e.g., Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli).

Capitalize genus; lowercase species. Abbreviate after first full mention (e.g., S. aureus).

Physician Assistant / Associate Capitalization

Capitalize Physician Assistant or Physician Associate only when referring to a formal program name or proper title (e.g., "University of Lynchburg DMSc Program"). Use lowercase for general occupational references (e.g., "The primary care physician assistant conducted the intake").

Preferred Usage & Academic Substitutions

Replacing Colloquialisms & Precise Clinical Distinction

Academic Term Replacement Table

Informal Phrase Academic Replacement Clinical Context Example
To bring to light Shed light on, elucidate, reveal This study helps elucidate the socioeconomic barriers...
A lot of / tons of Numerous, substantial, significant The trial yielded a substantial volume of patient data.
Level up Enhance, advance, improve Interventions aim to enhance functional mobility.
Bug going around Pathogen, viral outbreak, infection The viral pathogen spread throughout the regional facility.

Doctor vs. Physician Distinction

Doctor: Refers broadly to anyone holding a doctoral degree (DMSc, PhD, EdD, MD, DHSc).

Physician: Reserved specifically for licensed Doctors of Medicine (MD) or Osteopathic Medicine (DO).

Patient vs. Subject Terminology

Use patient when describing individuals undergoing clinical care. Use participant or subject in trial methodology research contexts.

Abbreviations & Acronyms (AMA Ch. 13.0)

First-Use Rules, Clinical Terms, & Units of Measure

First-Use Expansion Rule

Define abbreviations at their first appearance in the manuscript text by writing out the full term followed by the acronym in parentheses. Example: Doctor of Medical Science (DMSc) or World Health Organization (WHO).

Standard Medical Acronyms (No expansion needed):

DNA, RNA, HIV, AIDS, CT, MRI, ECG, ICU, IV, IM

Do Not Begin Sentences with Abbreviations:

Spell out the full word or restructure the sentence if an acronym starts a line.

Numbers & Units of Measure (AMA Ch. 18.0)

Numeral Rules, SI Units, Percentages, & P-Values

Use Numerals for Numbers 10+

Write 12 patients, 45 trials, 100 participants.

Spell Out Numbers Below 10

Write three clinicians, six visits, nine subjects.

Always Use Numerals with Units

Write 5 mg, 3 mL, 2 years old, 4%.

Statistical Reporting Guidelines

Italicize statistical symbols (P, N, r, t, F):

Outcomes differed significantly between treatment cohorts (P = .02; N = 142).

References & Citations (AMA Ch. 3.0)

In-Text Superscripts, Order, & Digital Object Identifiers (DOI)

In-Text Superscript Rules

  • Use Arabic superscript numerals consecutively (e.g., 1, 2-4, 1,3,5).
  • Place superscripts outside periods and commas.
  • Place superscripts inside colons and semicolons.
  • Do not insert spaces before superscript numbers.

Digital Object Identifier (DOI) Standard

Format DOIs cleanly without URL prefixes or trailing periods:

doi:10.1080/08959420.2011.605642

Ethical and Legal Standards (AMA Ch. 5.0)

IRB Approvals, HIPAA Privacy, & Academic Integrity

IRB & Human Subjects Protection

All research involving human subjects must state Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval or exemption in the methods section of the manuscript.

HIPAA & Patient Anonymity

Omit all identifying patient information (names, initials, hospital record numbers, exact admission dates) from manuscript figures and case reports.

AMA Reference Examples & Templates

Journals, Books, Online Resources, & Guidelines

Journal Article (Print)
Author(s). Article title. Abbrev Journal Name. Year;Vol(Issue):Pages.

Example: Smith T, Jones J. Type 1 diabetes management in youth. N Engl J Med. 2024;390(2):144-152.

Journal Article with DOI (Online)
Author(s). Article title. Abbrev Journal Name. Year;Vol(Issue):Pages. doi:10.xxxx/xxxxx

Example: Hunter RH, Sykes K. Environmental support for healthy aging. J Aging Soc Policy. 2023;35(4):354-371. doi:10.1080/08959420.2023.605642

AMA Reference Quick Helper

Core rules for DMSc Reference List entries:

  • Single-space within entries; double-space between entries.
  • Do NOT use hanging indents (AMA uses flush-left numbered entries).
  • List references in order of in-text numerical appearance.
  • Omit URLs if a valid Digital Object Identifier (DOI) is provided.
